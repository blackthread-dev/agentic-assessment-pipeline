# Discovery Call Transcript (fictional)

> Harbourline Heating & Cooling is a made-up company. This transcript was written to demonstrate the
> pipeline and does not describe any real business or person.

**Date:** 2026-09-10
**Participants:** Assessor; Dana Reyes (owner, Harbourline Heating & Cooling)

---

**Assessor:** Thanks for making time. Can you give me the quick version of the business?

**Dana:** Sure. We're residential heating and cooling. Furnace and AC installs, repairs, and we've got maybe three hundred and fifty customers on maintenance plans. Fourteen people total: nine techs, two in the office, a warehouse guy, my operations manager Sam, and me.

**Assessor:** What do you run the business on?

**Dana:** Jobber for scheduling and quotes. QuickBooks Online for the books. The phones are just a regular business line that rings the office. And a lot of spreadsheets, honestly. Sam has a maintenance plan spreadsheet that is basically his baby.

**Assessor:** What part of your week feels the most chaotic?

**Dana:** Mornings, and anything after four. In the fall, when the first cold snap hits, the phone does not stop. The two office people are booking, answering "when is my tech coming", and doing invoicing all at once. If they're on a call, the next call goes to voicemail. And people calling about a no-heat don't leave voicemails, they call the next company.

**Assessor:** Do you have a sense of how many calls you miss?

**Dana:** Our phone report said something like forty-something missed calls in a busy week last October. I don't know how many of those were new customers. Even if it's a handful, an install is five to twelve thousand dollars, so it stings.

**Assessor:** You mentioned "when is my tech coming". How often does that happen?

**Dana:** Constantly. I'd guess ten, fifteen calls a day in the busy season. The tech is running late, the customer calls the office, the office texts the tech, the tech doesn't answer because he's under a furnace, and then someone calls the customer back. It's the same loop every time.

**Assessor:** Walk me through what happens after a job is done.

**Dana:** Tech finishes, marks it complete in Jobber, and adds notes and parts. Then the office creates the invoice. Then Kim re-enters the invoice into QuickBooks, because the sync was set up years ago and it broke and nobody fixed it. So everything gets typed twice. She says it's about an hour a day, more at month-end. And sometimes the numbers don't match and we spend an afternoon figuring out why.

**Assessor:** And the maintenance plans?

**Dana:** That's Sam's spreadsheet. Every month he filters for who's due, then the office calls or emails them to book the tune-up. When it gets busy, that just doesn't happen. We had a bunch of plans lapse last year because nobody reached out for the renewal. Sam figured we lost maybe thirty plans. At about two hundred a year each, that's real money, and those are also the customers who buy the next furnace from us.

**Assessor:** How do quotes work?

**Dana:** For installs, a comfort advisor goes out, then writes the quote that evening at the kitchen table. Some quotes take two or three days to go out. We don't really follow up on them. If they don't answer, they don't answer.

**Assessor:** Anything with reporting?

**Dana:** Every Monday Sam builds a report on revenue per tech, callbacks, that kind of thing. Pulls from Jobber, pastes into Excel. Takes him most of the morning. I look at it, but honestly I'd want it quicker, not fancier.

**Assessor:** Is there anything you've tried already?

**Dana:** We looked at one of the big all-in-one field service platforms but it was a lot of money and a six-month migration. I don't want to rip everything out. Jobber works. I think we're just not using half of it.

**Assessor:** If one thing improved by next month, what would you pick?

**Dana:** The phones. Not missing people, and not having the office answer "where's my tech" all day. Then the double entry, because that one makes Kim want to quit.

**Assessor:** Any data we need to be careful with?

**Dana:** Customer addresses and phone numbers. Card payments go through Jobber, we don't store cards ourselves.

**Assessor:** That's really helpful. I'll turn this into a short assessment with what I'd fix first.
